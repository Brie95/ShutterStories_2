Understanding camera equipment is the foundation of strong photography. The right tools help you control light, sharpen your images, and capture the exact mood you want. This section introduces the essential gear every photographer should know, whether you're shooting portraits, travel scenes, or landscapes.

## Camera Bodies
The camera body is the core of your setup. It houses the sensor, controls, and processing system. Beginners often start with entry‑level DSLRs or mirrorless cameras, while advanced photographers choose bodies with faster autofocus, better low‑light performance, and weather sealing.

See how camera bodies affect portrait work in [[portrait-photography]].

## Lenses
Lenses shape the look of your photos more than any other piece of equipment.  
- **Prime lenses** offer sharp images and wide apertures.  
- **Zoom lenses** provide flexibility for travel and everyday shooting.  
- **Wide‑angle lenses** are great for landscapes and city photography.  
- **Telephoto lenses** help isolate subjects and compress backgrounds.

Learn how lenses impact composition in [[photography-techniques]].

## Tripods & Stabilizers
Tripods keep your camera steady for long exposures, night photography, and landscape shots. Handheld stabilizers or gimbals help create smooth video and reduce motion blur when shooting on the move.

See examples of stable travel shots in [[travel-photography]].

## Memory Cards & Storage
Fast, reliable memory cards ensure your camera can save photos quickly, especially when shooting in RAW or burst mode. External drives or cloud storage help you organize and back up your work safely.

Learn how storage affects editing workflow in [[photo-editing]].

## Lighting Tools
Lighting equipment includes reflectors, diffusers, LED panels, and external flashes. These tools help you shape light, soften shadows, and highlight your subject — especially in portrait photography.

Explore lighting techniques in [[photography-techniques]].

---

Camera equipment is the backbone of every photographer’s workflow. Choosing the right tools and understanding how they work will improve your images across all styles, from portraits to travel photography.
