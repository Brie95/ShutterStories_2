# **Organizing System Analysis: Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History Online Collection**

  

## **Overview**

  

The Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History (NMNH) maintains one of the world’s largest public digital collections, offering access to millions of specimens, artifacts, and research materials. Its organizing system is designed to support scientific inquiry, public education, and open exploration. The structure blends hierarchical taxonomies, faceted metadata, and rich relational linking to help users navigate an enormous and diverse dataset.

  

## **Primary Resources Being Organized**

  

The NMNH online collection primarily organizes **digitized specimens and artifacts**, including:

  

- **Biological specimens** (mammals, insects, plants)

- **Geological samples** (minerals, fossils, meteorites)

- **Anthropological artifacts** (tools, cultural objects, textiles)

- **High‑resolution specimen images**

- **Metadata records** containing scientific names, collection dates, geographic origins, and catalog numbers

  

Each resource is represented as a structured digital record, often accompanied by photographs, taxonomic information, and collection notes. These records serve both scientific and educational purposes.

  

## **Primary Interactions the System Supports**

  

### **Research and Data Retrieval**

  

Researchers can search for specimens using filters such as taxonomy, geographic region, collector, or date. For example, a scientist studying beetles can filter by _Order: Coleoptera_ and retrieve thousands of relevant records.

  

### **Browsing and Discovery**

  

Casual users can explore thematic categories such as “Fossils,” “Birds,” or “Human Origins.” The interface encourages exploration through visual galleries and curated collections.

  

### **Verification and Reference**

  

Each record includes authoritative metadata, making the system useful for fact‑checking scientific names, collection locations, and specimen histories.

  

### **Educational Engagement**

  

Teachers and learners can access images, descriptions, and contextual information for classroom activities or personal learning.

  

## **Classification System Used**

  

### **Hierarchical Classification**

  

Biological specimens follow scientific taxonomy:

  

**Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

  

For example, a record for the African lion appears under:

  

- _Kingdom: Animalia_

- _Class: Mammalia_

- _Order: Carnivora_

  

This hierarchy is effective for researchers who rely on standardized scientific organization.

  

### **Faceted Classification**

  

Users can filter by multiple facets, including:

  

- **Geographic origin** (e.g., “Kenya,” “Pacific Ocean”)

- **Collection date**

- **Collector name**

- **Specimen type** (holotype, fossil, mineral)

  

Faceted navigation is highly effective for non‑experts because it allows multiple entry points into the collection.

  

## **Representation of Relationships Between Resources**

  

### **Hyperlinks**

  

Each specimen record links to related taxonomic groups, similar specimens, or collection events.

  

### **Categories and Tags**

  

Records include categories such as “Birds,” “Fossils,” or “Cultural Artifacts,” helping users browse by theme.

  

### **Structured Metadata**

  

Fields like “Collected by,” “Found in,” or “Related specimens” create semantic connections across the database.

  

### **Search Filters**

  

Faceted filters implicitly represent relationships by grouping items that share attributes (e.g., all specimens collected in Madagascar).

  

## **Conclusion**

  

The Smithsonian NMNH online collection uses a robust organizing system that blends hierarchical taxonomy with flexible faceted metadata. Its structure supports both expert research and casual exploration, making it an effective and accessible digital collection for a global audience.
